# Plane structures (`strix-structure`)

> **Status:** done — stage 1 (solver, structure object, diagrams, commands), stage 2 (Structures tab, Structure Editor pane with its tables), stage 3 (the written working), stage 4 (dimensions, stations, the full-page editor), the structure exercise and the report/exercise modes · **Last updated:** 2026-11-19 · **Crates:** `crates/structure` (`L1`), `crates/structure/src/working.rs`, `crates/engine/src/cmd/structure.rs` and `cmd/structure/working.rs` (`L4`), `crates/ui-egui/src/structure_pane.rs`, `crates/ui-egui/src/ribbon/structures.rs` (`L6`)

Strix documents hold plane structures — beams, frames and trusses — that are solved where they sit and drawn with their reactions and their N, V (T) and M diagrams one under the other, at the same horizontal scale, as textbooks draw them. Lengths and loads may be calculation parameters (`L`, `q`), so a structure follows the calculations before it and each exercise variant has its own numbers and diagrams. This is a Strix feature beyond Word parity; the model is the one of 2D frame analysis tools such as structural-analyser.com, drawn with the conventions of Italian structural mechanics texts.

## Model (`crates/structure`, L1, depends on `strix-calc` and `strix-plot`)

A `Structure` (JSON, camelCase) has:

- **Units:** `lengthUnit` (`m` default, `cm`, `mm`) and `forceUnit` (`kN` default, `N`, `daN`); moments are force × length, distributed loads force / length. Changing a unit (`structure.set`, Structures › Properties › Units) keeps the structure as it is and rewrites its numbers: a 1 m beam becomes 100 cm, 10 kN/m becomes 0.1 kN/cm, `E`, `I`, `area` follow, angles stay (`Structure::convert_units`, rounded to 12 significant digits); an expression is kept as written and takes its value in the new units (a parameter with a unit, `L = 6 m`, converts by itself; a plain number counts in the new unit). With `"convert": false`, or with a `structure` given in the same call, the unit is only relabelled. The editor's view and grid follow, so the sheet shows the same thing. Coordinates are `y` up, angles in degrees counter-clockwise.
- **Nodes** `{id, x, y, hinge?}`: `hinge` makes every member end meeting there free to turn (an internal hinge, Gerber beams).
- **Members** `{id, a, b, hingeA?, hingeB?, e?, i?, area?}`: the local axis `x` goes from `a` to `b`, `y` is turned 90° counter-clockwise; the **reference fibres** are on the `−y` side (below a member drawn left to right, on the right of a column drawn upwards). `hingeA`/`hingeB` release one end. Without `e`, `i`, `area`: `EI = 1` (constant along the structure) and the member does not stretch (`EA = 10⁶·EI/ℓ²`, `ℓ` the longest member), which is what textbook hyperstatic frames assume; isostatic results never depend on stiffness.
- **Supports** `{node, kind, angle?}`: `fixed` (incastro), `pin` (cerniera), `roller` (carrello, a triangle on wheels), `rollerCircle` (the same carrello drawn as a circle under the node on the ground: restrains like a roller), `link` (pendolo: restrains like a roller, drawn as a bar), `slider` (glifo / bipendolo: the translation across its plane and the rotation). `angle` turns the support about its node: at 0 it stands on the ground below the node, at 90 it is on a wall to its right, at −90 on a wall to its left (a cantilever's fixed end).
- **Loads** (`type`):
  - `force {node | member + at, value, angle}` — `angle` −90 (down) by default; `at` from the member's `a` end;
  - `moment {node | member + at, value}` — counter-clockwise positive;
  - `distributed {member, q1, q2?, from?, to?, angle?, axes?, per?}` — linear from `q1` to `q2` (uniform, triangular, trapezoidal) over `[from, to]` (the whole member by default); `axes: "global"` (default; `angle` from the horizontal) or `"local"` (`angle` from the member's axis, −90 towards the reference fibres: wind on a column); `per: "length"` (default, per metre of member) or `"projection"` (per metre of the member's projection across the load: snow on a rafter).
  - **Names** (`load_names`, `crates/structure/src/names.rs`): each load is named by its kind — `F` concentrated forces, `q` distributed loads, `C` couples — and one count runs through all of them (`q_1`, `F_2`, `C_3`…), the loads taken by their place: left to right (a distributed load by its stretch's leftmost end), then top to bottom, then force, distributed load, couple (then as listed); a distributed load that varies names its ends, `q_(1,1)` and `q_(1,2)` (`q₁,₁`, `q₁,₂` on the drawing). A load that does nothing (zero, or nowhere) is skipped. The drawing writes the name before the value (`F₁ = 12 kN`), the working defines the names and its equations use them.
- **Every number is a `Num`**: a JSON number, or an expression of the calculation parameters (`"L/2"`, `"q"`). A parameter with a unit converts to the structure's units (`L = 450 cm` is 4.5 in a metre structure, `q = 12 kN/m` is 12, `30°` is 30 degrees); a plain number counts in the structure's units; `L/2 - 1` mixes a length and a number, so lengths count as plain numbers in the structure's unit there, as coordinates do in a CAD drawing. A failed expression keeps its last value and is reported.
- **Diagrams** `{reactions, n, v, m, values, hatch, signs, dimensions, stations}`: each of `n`, `v`, `m` has `show`, `positive` (`up`: the member's `+y` side; `down`: the reference fibres' side) and `amplitude` (the largest value's height, paper mm, 12 by default). **Italian convention** (default, `structure.set {"convention": "italian"}`): N and V positive up, M positive down, so the moment is drawn on the stretched fibres. `titles {n, v, m}` are the diagrams' symbols (`N`, `T`, `M` by default; `V` for English texts), written over the panels and in the working. **Names** `{n, v, m}` (empty by default, up to 40 characters) are written over the panels in place of the symbols (`Sforzo normale [kN]`, `Sforzo di taglio [kN]`, `Momento flettente [kN·m]`); the working, the questions and the properties keep the symbols, and an empty name gives the symbol back. On the Structures tab they are the **Diagram Names** group, with Full Names (Axial force, Shear force, Bending moment in the interface's language) and Symbols.
- `title`, `width` (paper mm, 160), `answer` (true: reactions and diagrams are the answer, hidden in the student copy).
- `view` (`all` by default, not written; `structure`: the structure with its loads only; `reactions`: the reactions only; `diagrams`: the diagrams only; `axial`, `shear`, `moment`: one diagram only, whatever its flag — a truss's `axial` its axial forces; `equilibrium`: the free body the reactions' working balances; `section`: one stretch of the working cut, with `cut {member, from, to, tag}`; `arms`: the lever arms of one moment equation, with `pivot {node, members}` — see [Working](#working)) and `group` (a name the drawings of one structure share, `structure-1`…: see [Exercise](#exercise)).

Sign conventions: N positive in tension; V positive when it turns the piece clockwise; M positive when it stretches the reference fibres.

## Analysis

- **Classification** by the rank of the equilibrium matrix (equations: two translations per node and the rotations; unknowns: three internal forces per member — N and the two end moments — and one per constraint): **isostatic**, **hyperstatic of degree h** (unknowns − rank), or **labile** (rank < equations, the number of independent mechanisms). Three hinges in a row, two parallel rollers, a couple on a node where every member is hinged: labile, whatever the count of constraints says.
- **Solution** by the stiffness method: rotations are one per node for the member ends rigidly joined there, one of its own for each hinged end; supports are constraints solved with Lagrange multipliers, which are the reactions. Member loads enter as consistent nodal loads (exact for a clamped beam), and the internal forces along each member follow by equilibrium of the piece from its `a` end, so a stretch's law is exact (polynomial), not sampled from the solver.
- **Truss:** when every member is hinged at both ends and carries no load along it, the structure is a truss: one panel shows each member's axial force (tension positive, coral; compression blue; zero members dashed grey) instead of the N, V, M diagrams.
- **Notable values** (`notables`): at the ends of each stretch (members' ends, loads' positions, with the jumps under concentrated loads), the extremes of M where V is zero, and the zeros of each diagram.

## Drawing

`Structure::render_for(ws, show_answers)` returns a `strix-plot` `PlotRendered`, so the engine draws it, writes its SVG and prints it as it does a plot. Panels, one under the other at one horizontal scale, the ones its `view` shows:

1. the **structure** with its supports (Italian textbook symbols: hatched ground, rollers on wheels, links as bars with hinges, fixed ends hatched), hinges as white circles, node names away from the members, and its **loads** in red: arrows with their names and values (`q₁ = 10 kN/m`, `F₂ = 12 kN`, a varying load's ends `q₃,₁ = 2 kN/m`, `q₃,₂ = 5 kN/m`), curved arrows for couples, distributed loads as rows of arrows proportional to the intensity with their envelope and values;
2. the **reactions** on the structure drawn in grey: components along the axes (along the support's direction for a roller, link or slider), arrows pointing the way the force acts, their magnitudes, curved arrows for moments; a component that came out negative also has, beside its arrow on the side away from the structure, the way it was taken positive (along x, along y, the roller's direction, a couple round the real one, each the way the structure's `positive` senses count positive: +x, +y, counter-clockwise by default, see [Working](#working)) faded and crossed out with a red ✕. Drawn on its own (`view: reactions`, an exercise's answer to its reactions part) it carries the structure's indications too: the loads with their names and values, the forces' angles and the members' slopes, the dimensions (after the reactions, so the chains go under their arrows and values) and the node names (on the side no reaction's arrow lies along, farther out round a fixed end's couple);
3. **N**, **V (T)** and **M** (or the truss panel), each in its colour (blue, teal, plum), hatched across the axis, with ⊕/⊖ in each area and the notable values written at the curve. Beside each title, the sign convention its values follow on a short outlined member with two zigzag cut faces ([`render/conventions.rs`](../crates/structure/src/render/conventions.rs)): N positive pulling (arrows outwards), T positive turning the piece clockwise (up on the left face, down on the right), M positive stretching the reference fibres (dashed inside the member near its lower edge; clockwise on the left face, counter-clockwise on the right).

What the drawing measures (`crates/structure/src/render/annotate.rs`):

- **Dimensions** (`diagrams.dimensions`; Structures › Show › Dimensions; on in a new structure, written out, off when the key is absent, so a structure saved before it keeps its drawing and its size on the page), on the structure's panel: chains of distances in grey below the structure (x) — first with the points where loads act, start and end on level members, when they add any, then between the nodes, then overall when there are more than two stations — and at its right (y, with the loads on plumb members; a grid's cell keeps room for it), texts along their lines. The chains keep clear of everything drawn before them: the first goes 4.5 mm under the lowest support, load, arrow or name (a force pointing up from under the beam, with its name), the chain of heights as far right of the rightmost, and each extension line starts 1 mm under (beside) what is drawn across it, so none crosses a support or a load; loads on an inclined member get a chain parallel to it on its reference fibres' side, distances measured along the member from its first node (as `at`, `from`, `to` are). Each **inclined member's slope** is marked once per value: an arc from a dashed horizontal at its lower end, with the angle. Each **inclined force** (or inclined distributed load) gets the angle it makes with its member: an arc from the member's side nearest the arrow's tail to the arrow, with the angle, in the loads' red; a force along or across its member, or at a node where it runs along or across a member, is evident and gets none.
- **Stations** (`diagrams.stations`; Structures › Show › Stations; on in a new structure, off when absent, as dimensions), on each diagram: a tick on each member's axis where a notable value is inside the member (a load's position, a jump, M's extreme, a zero), a dotted ordinate up to the curve, and the distance from the member's first node (`2.333 m`) written across the axis on the side away from the diagram; one too close to the last on paper keeps only its tick.
- **Values' direction:** a value that belongs to a point (a member's end, a jump, an extreme) is written across the member, standing on the curve there (vertical on a beam); a stretch of constant value keeps one level value at its middle. Rotated texts are `PlotPrim::TextAt` (centred, turned), drawn the same in the document, its SVG and the editor.

A structure that is not solved (labile, invalid) shows the structure and a red line saying why. The student copy (`exercise.show` off, `answer` on) keeps every panel and the structure, with the reactions and diagrams left out, so both copies have the same size.

**Layout.** Each panel is drawn on its own and measured with everything on it — texts (estimated at 0.55 of their height per character), arrows, dimension chains. The scale is the largest that keeps every panel inside `width` (and the structure at most 70 mm tall, 60 in a grid): the structure is drawn, measured and narrowed until nothing sticks out (a reaction's value at the left, the chain of heights at the right), a few rounds at most. The panels are then centred, each column of a grid on its own, and stacked by their real height, 5 mm apart, a diagram's title and sign convention on a row of their own 11 mm tall above it; there are no lines or frames between or round the panels. The student copy is laid out from the teacher's, so both have the same size. Texts keep 1 mm from the line they name; a diagram's values, stations and ⊕/⊖ treat the curves (sampled every 0.8 mm) and each other as obstacles: each tries several places (farther from the curve, a little along the axis either way, a station beside its tick) and takes the first clear one, else the one over the fewest things.

## Commands

| Command | Does |
|---|---|
| `structure.insert {structure?, template?, openPane?}` | inserts a structure at the caret: `beam` (default: simply supported, 6 m, 10 kN/m), `cantilever`, `continuous`, `frame` (portal), `truss`; `openPane` opens the Structure Editor |
| `structure.get` | the source and `resolved` (its numbers' values), `status` (`isostatic`, `hyperstatic`, `labile`, `invalid`, `empty`), `degree`, `message`, `truss`, `reactions`, each member's length and notable N, V, M values, `errors` |
| `structure.set {structure?, title?, width?, lengthUnit?, forceUnit?, convert?, answer?, convention?, diagrams?, titles?, names?, layout?}` | settings (`diagrams`, `titles` and `names` merged key by key; `layout` `auto`, `column` or `grid`) |
| `structure.node {id?, x?, y?, hinge?, rename?, remove?}` | adds (next free letter), changes, renames (everywhere) or removes a node with its members, supports and loads |
| `structure.member {id?, a?, b?, hingeA?, hingeB?, e?, i?, area?, remove?}` | adds, changes or removes a member (with its loads); `null` clears `e`, `i`, `area` |
| `structure.support {node, kind?, angle?, remove?}` | one support per node: puts, changes or removes it |
| `structure.load {index?, load?, remove?}` | adds, replaces or removes a load |
| `structure.working {labels?}` | writes the solution after the structure as live calculations ([Working](#working)) |
| `structure.exercise {structure?, template?, labels?, questions?}` | a new exercise on a structure ([Exercise](#exercise)) → `{exercise, group}` |

## The Structures tab (Strutture)

Between Math and Teaching (`ribbon::TABS`; a saved ribbon layout gets it after Math). Its groups:

- **Structure:** Structure (a menu of the templates: Simply Supported Beam, Cantilever, Continuous Beam, Portal Frame, Truss; it inserts one and opens the full-page editor on it, `ui.structureNew {template?}`), Structure Exercise, **Structure Editor** (the full-page editor, `ui.structureEdit`; Close Editor while it is open, `ui.structureClose`) — one editor button: the side pane is still `view.structurePane` (command search, View) — and **To Exercise** / **To Report** (`structure.mode`, see [Modes](#modes)).
- **Properties:** the structure's **Title** (a box) and **Units** (m, cm, mm; kN, daN, N).
- **Diagrams:** Reactions, Axial Force N, Shear T (the shear's title as the structure names it), Moment M, each shown or hidden; **Positive Side** (up or down for each of N, T, M); **Italian Convention**.
- **Diagram Names:** a box beside each of N, T, M (the panels' names, [Model](#model-crates-structure-l1-depends-on-strix-calc-and-strix-plot)), **Full Names** and **Symbols**.
- **Positive Senses:** x Right/Left, y Up/Down, Rotation Counter-clockwise/Clockwise ([Working](#working)).
- **Show:** Values, Hatching, Signs, Dimensions, Stations; **Panel Layout** (Automatic — frames in two columns —, One Column, Two Columns).

Each change writes the working again at once, in a report and in an exercise; a new shear symbol (`titles.v`) is also written in the exercise's questions (`… the shear force V.`) and in the working's sign conventions, in the same undo step. The questions name the shear as the laws do (`T_1(x)`, `V_1(x)`: the title's letters, `T` when it has none).
- **Teaching:** **Solution Only** (`answer`: reactions and diagrams hidden in the student copy).

Everything but Structure and the editor needs a structure selected (`structure.set` is enabled only then).

## The Structure Editor pane

A task pane (docked right, 440 pt) for the selected structure, every change made through the `structure.*` commands (so undoable and scriptable):

- **Status:** isostatic, hyperstatic of degree h, labile (with its degrees of freedom), a truss; the expressions that failed and the analysis' warnings.
- **Sheet:** the structure as the document draws it (`Structure::schema_prims`: the same symbols, loads, names, dimensions, slopes and force angles) on a white sheet with a grid; the wheel zooms about the pointer, the middle or right button pans, **Fit** shows it whole. The pointer snaps to the nodes, and to the grid with **Snap** (its step in **Grid**); its coordinates are shown in the corner. **Dimensions** (on by default, the editor's own view: the document draws them as Structures › Show › Dimensions says) shows the chains of dimensions, the inclined members' slopes and the angles inclined forces make with their members, at the same paper size whatever the zoom. Angles are drawn as the dimensions are: a thin grey extension line along the reference (the horizontal, the member), an arc in the dimension lines' colour with an empty circle at each end, and the angle beside it — on the page too.
- **Full page:** a **double-click** on a structure in the document (or Edit Structure, or the pane's Edit in Full Page) opens the editor **in place of the page**, like the CAD editor (a drawing of the structure's written working — its free body, a stretch — opens the structure itself, since a change writes the working and its drawings again; `structure.*` edits with such a drawing selected are made to the structure too, which stays selected): the sheet as large as the window with the typed point below it, the selection's properties and the tables at the right, and the **Structure Editor** ribbon tab brought up (a contextual tab, `structure_pane::EDITOR_TAB`) with the tools: *Selection* (Select, Rotate, Erase), *Draw* (Node, Member), *Supports* (Fixed, Pin, Roller, Roller (circle), Link, Slider, Internal hinge), *Loads* (Force, Couple, Distributed), *Sheet* (Snap, Grid, Fit, Dimensions), *Teaching* (Write Working) and *Close* (Close Editor) — `ui.structureTool {tool}`, `ui.structureSnap {on?}`, `ui.structureGrid {size}`, `ui.structureFit`, `ui.structureDimensions {on?}`. Single-letter shortcuts switch tools while the sheet has the keyboard (`V` Select, `R` Rotate, `N` Node, `M` Member, `F` Force, `C` Couple, `D` Distributed, `H` Hinge, `E` Erase). Whenever a tool other than Select is active, an **active-command banner** sits above the sheet (with the tool's icon, name, step-by-step instruction and a `×` button back to Select), the sheet frame takes the tool's accent colour (coral for Erase), and the pointer switches to a crosshair or pointing hand over valid targets. The pane keeps its own compact toolbar. Close Editor, Esc with nothing selected, or a structure no longer selected (undone, deleted) goes back to the page (which takes the keyboard again) and the tab that was open. The document's panes are hidden meanwhile; one editor takes the page at a time (a CAD drawing does not open over it).
- **Selection** (Select tool): a click picks a node, else a load (its arrow, its couple's circle, its trapezoid/triangle band scaled to `q1`, `q2`), else a node's support symbol, else a member, and shows what the pointer is over; **Alt+click** (or **Tab** over the sheet) cycles through overlapping items at the pointer; **Shift** or **Ctrl**+click adds or takes out; a drag from an empty place draws a **window**: dragged left to right (blue, solid) it takes the nodes in it, the members with both ends in it and the loads in it; dragged right to left (green, dashed) it takes whatever it touches, members crossing it included (Shift keeps what was selected); a **double-click** selects the whole connected part (its nodes and members); **Ctrl+A** selects every node, member and load. A small tag by the pointer names what it is over with its symbol and main figures (`Node A (0; 3 m) · Pin`, `Member 1 (A → B) · 6 m · 30°`, `F₁ · 10 kN · −90°`, `q₂ · 10 kN/m`), and a selected member marks its `a → b` direction and dashed reference fibres on the sheet. **Moving:** dragging a node or a member moves the whole selection (the selected nodes and the ends of the selected members), the node grabbed (a member's end nearest the pointer) landing on the grid when snapping, the Δx, Δy shown in a translucent pill in the corner, as one undo step (`structure.set`); a coordinate written as an expression stays one, the move's step kept as one number at its end (`L` → `L + 0.5` → `L + 1`, back to `L`). Dragging a force or couple on a member slides it along the member (its `at`, on the grid; an expression stays one, the same way); a drag that ends where it started changes nothing. The **arrow keys** move the selection by the grid (Shift: ten steps). **Delete** (or Backspace) removes it, nodes with their members, supports and loads, in one step. **Esc** goes one step per press: it cancels the drag or the member being drawn, then goes back to Select, then clears the selection, and only then leaves the full-page editor. The sheet takes these keys (the page and the program's shortcuts do not see them): in the pane once it is clicked, or while the pointer is over it and nothing else has the keyboard; in the full-page editor whenever no box is being typed in, where Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y undo and redo as on the page. Loads are selected by their place in the list, so a change in their number (an undo, a table, a command) takes them out of the selection.
- **Grips and graphical vector rotation** (`structure_pane/grips.rs`, `structure_pane/rotate.rs`): what is selected shows small handles, dragged to edit it graphically in one undo step, the pointer a hand over them. A **node's** square (also at both ends of a selected member; with one node or member selected, since with several a drag moves them all) moves that node alone, onto the grid when snapping (Shift keeps its row or column); dropped on another node (a red ring marks it) it **joins** it: members, support and loads go to that node (which keeps its own support), a member left with no length or doubling another goes. A **point load's** square (a force's point, a couple's centre) carries it onto any node or member (at the point under the pointer, rounded to the grid); a force at a node can be dragged by its arrow the same way. **Vector inclination** works both through grips — a **force's** round handle at its tail, a **distributed load's** round handle above the middle of its envelope, a **support's** round handle below its ground symbol — and through the dedicated **Rotate** tool (`ui.structureTool {"tool": "rotate"}`, shortcut `R`, or the *Rotate* button in Properties and the right-click menu): clicking or dragging any force, distributed load or support opens a full **360° graduated protractor** on the sheet centred at the pivot, with 15° ticks, cardinal degree labels in the item's own angle convention (global or local), teal reference ticks along (`‖`) and across (`∟`) the connected member(s), a shaded sector from the ghosted starting ray to the live ray, a draggable rim knob, and a live readout tag (`F₁: −60° (∟)`). With Snap on, the ray snaps within 5° to connected members' `∟`/`‖` directions and to 15° steps (holding Shift switches to 1° steps; with Snap off, Shift enables snapping). Expressions are preserved when turned by a step (`alpha` → `alpha + 15`). A **distributed load's** diamonds set where it starts and stops along its member (never shorter than a grid step; at the member's end it is "to the end" again).
- **Context menu** (a right click, `structure_pane/menu.rs`; a right click on something not selected selects it first; while a member is drawn a right click only ends it; the right button still pans when dragged): on a **node** — Support (the six kinds or None, plus Rotate on an existing support), Internal hinge, **Join the members** (where exactly two aligned members meet with no support or hinge and the same section; otherwise disabled with a tooltip saying why), Add a force / couple here, Draw a member from here, Select its loads, Select the connected part, Delete; on a **member** — Add a force / couple here (at the point clicked, on the grid), Add a distributed load, **Split here** / **Split in half**, **Swap ends (A ↔ B)** (`edits::reverse_member`: flips `a` and `b`, end hinges and reference fibres while keeping every load at the same physical place and world direction), Hinge at end A / B, Select its ends / loads / the connected part, Delete; on a **force** — Rotate, Reverse (an expression `F` becomes `-(F)` and back), Direction (Down −90°, Up 90°, Right 0°, Left 180°, At −45°, At 45°, Perpendicular to the member, Along the member), Delete; on a **couple** — Reverse, Delete; on a **distributed load** — Rotate, Reverse, Uniform, Triangular, On the whole member, Perpendicular to the member (local axes), Along the member (local axes), Delete; on **several things** — Join the members, Select their loads, Deselect, Delete; on the **empty sheet** — Add a node here, Draw a member from here, Select all, Fit, Snap to the grid, Dimensions. With another tool than Select, Select tool comes first.
- **Tools:** Select and Rotate (see above), Node, Member (two clicks, then it goes on from its end with a live `L, α°` badge; Shift constrains to 0°/45°/90°; Esc or a right click stops; missing nodes are made), the six supports (a click on a node sets it and selects the node; clicking and dragging aims its angle straight away), Internal hinge (a click on a node toggles its hinge, or near a member's end toggles `hingeA`/`hingeB`), Force (click to place downward, or click and drag to aim its vector angle with the protractor) and Couple (on a node, or on a member at the point clicked, rounded to the grid), Distributed (a uniform load on the member clicked, selecting the new load), Erase (removes a single load or a node's support when clicked directly, or a node with what uses it, or a member with its loads, previewing in coral what will be erased). New loads start at 10 in the structure's units.
- **Point:** coordinates typed as in the CAD editor — `x, y`, or `x; y` with decimal commas, `@dx, dy` relative, or polar `@L<α` (`@4<30`) from the member's last point or the selected node — place a node (or the member's next point with Member) on Enter; with a rotatable force, distributed load or support selected (or with Rotate active), `α` sets its angle and `@Δα` shifts it.
- **Properties:** in titled sections, label, box and unit lined up, showing `= value` beside any parameter expression (`L/2` → `= 3 m`) and selecting newly placed items automatically. *Node:* name (renamed everywhere), x, y, internal hinge, the members joined there; *Support:* kind, angle with an interactive orientation bar (`0°`, `−90°`, `90°`, `180°`, `−15°`, `+15°`, `‖`, `∟`, `Rotate`), and solved **Reactions** (`H_A`, `V_A`, `M_A` or `R_B`) when available; *Loads:* those on the node by symbol (`F₁`, `C₂`, clickable) and Add Force/Couple there. *Member:* its ends (chosen from the nodes) with a `↔` button to swap `A ↔ B`, each end's hinge, length and slope, and solved **Internal forces** (N, T, M min/max ranges); *Section:* E, I, A with their units (empty: none); *Loads:* those on the member and Add Force/Couple/Distributed Load on it. *Load:* named by its symbol (`F₁`, `q₂`, `C₃`), where it acts, its position, values, an interactive **vector compass dial** (64×64 pt) with preset buttons (`↓ −90°`, `↑ 90°`, `→ 0°`, `← 180°`, `−45°`, `+45°`, `−15°`, `+15°`, `∟`, `‖`, `↔`, `Rotate`) and live Cartesian (`F_x, F_y` / `q_x, q_y`) and member (`F‖, F∟`) components, then its options. *Several selected:* counts and clickable chips for each selected node, member and load (Shift-click removes one from the selection, click focuses it), plus Remove Selected. *Nothing selected:* how many nodes, members, supports and loads it has and the solved reactions (its title, units, diagram names and positive senses are on the Structures tab).
- **Tables** (closed at first, each with its count): Nodes (x, y, hinge), Members (ends, hinges, E, I, A), Supports (node clickable to select, kind, angle), Loads (symbol button `F₁`/`q₂`/`C₃` to select, where, position, values, direction, from the member's axis, per projection), each row editable and removable, with Add buttons. Every number may be a calculation parameter or an expression; a plain number is read with a decimal point or comma.

## Working

`structure.working` (Structures › Teaching › Write Working, or the editor's button) writes after the selected structure its solution as live calculations, in the interface's language (`labels`):

- **Positive senses.** The structure's `positive {x: right|left, y: up|down, rotation: ccw|cw}` (right, up, counter-clockwise by default; not written then; `structure.set {"positive": {...}}`, merged; Structures › *Positive Senses*) are the senses the working counts positive: the equations sum forces and moments so, the reactions are taken positive so (`V_A` pointing down with `y: down`, `M_A` clockwise with `rotation: cw`) and come out negative when they act the other way, the laws of N, T, M unchanged. The free body draws them as a symbol in the row above it — an arrow along x and one along y from a common origin, named at their tips, with bold heads, and a curved arrow marked `+` inside the quadrant between them (where x and y both count positive), open towards their origin, with the same stroke and the same filled head as the axes' arrows ([`Pen::positive`](../crates/structure/src/render/conventions.rs)) — and its reactions' arrows point so; the reactions' panel crosses out a component negative in them.
- **Reactions.** One unknown per support component: `H_A`, `V_A` (along x and y), `R_B` along an inclined roller's or link's direction (`H_B` or `V_B` when it is horizontal or vertical), `M_A` for a fixed end or slider. The equations come from equilibrium — rotation about each support (`ΣM_A = 0`), translation along x and y (`ΣF_x = 0`, `ΣF_y = 0`), and the rotation about an internal hinge of the part on one side of it (`ΣM_B = 0 (part about the hinge B, Member 2)`) — and are written in an order where each has one unknown left, a translation preferred whenever it isolates one (the second vertical reaction of a beam from `ΣF_y = 0` once `ΣM_A = 0` gave the first, rather than a second moment equation), so calc solves it and shows its steps one under the other (`{"layout": "lines"}`): `0 kN·m = V_B·6 m − F_1·d_(1,A) ⇒ V_B·6 m = F_1·d_(1,A) ⇒ … ⇒ V_B = 30 kN` (a translation's both sides turned so the first unknown's term is positive, a rotation's kept as the sense it counts positive gives it: clockwise, `0 kN·m = -V_B·6 m + F_1·d_(1,A) ⇒ -V_B·6 m = -F_1·d_(1,A) ⇒ … ⇒ V_B = F_1·(d_(1,A))/(6 m)`; an unknown alone with no load is `H_A = 0 kN`). Each reaction solved is a calculation name the later equations use. First, right under the working's heading (before *Loads* and the equations), the working draws the **free body** its equations balance (a drawing of the structure with `view: equilibrium`, in its group, written again with the working; [`render/equilibrium.rs`](../crates/structure/src/render/equilibrium.rs)): the supports faded and their reactions drawn as the unknowns (`H_A`, `V_A`, `V_B`, `R_B`, `M_A`, pointing the way they count positive), each distributed load faded with its resultant at its centroid (`F_1`; a trapezoid's `F_(2,1)` and `F_(2,2)` at a half and two thirds), each askew force faded with its horizontal and vertical parts (`F_(3,h)`, `F_(3,v)`, as long as their share of it), couples by their names, everything named as the equations name it and no values; and the dimensions (whatever Show › Dimensions says) with a station at each resultant, so each moment's arm `d` reads off the drawing. An exercise's reactions part has it too. Loads enter by their names, defined first under *Loads* with their sizes (`F_1 = 12 kN`, `q_1 = 10 kN/m`, `q_(2,1) = 2 kN/m`; the equations' signs give the directions), as resultants. **The quantities are prepared first, in variables of their own**, so each moment equation is written algebraically and stays short: under the loads, each distributed load's resultant `F_n` as its area (`F_1 = q_1·6 m = 60 kN`; a trapezoid as a rectangle and a triangle, `F_(2,1) = q_(2,1)·6 m`, `F_(2,2) = (q_(2,2) − q_(2,1))·(6 m)/2`; a load per projection over the projected length), then each force acting askew — concentrated or a resultant — split into its horizontal and vertical parts (`F_(3,h) = F_3·cos(30°)`, `F_(3,v) = F_3·sin(30°)`; a numeric factor for an angle with no short value), which the translations use too (`ΣF_x`: `H_A − F_(3,h)`). Under each rotation's heading, before its equation, the **arm** of each force about that point, named `d` after the force and the point (`d_(1,A)`, `d_(2,1,A)`, `d_(3,v,A)` for the vertical part's horizontal arm, `d_(3,h,A)` for the horizontal part's vertical one) and worked out at once (`d_(1,B) = 6 m − (3 m)/2 = 4.5 m`), each once (a hinge's part reuses its support's); the equation then reads `0 kN·m = V_B·6 m − F_1·d_(1,A) − F_(3,v)·d_(3,v,A) + C_4`. The reactions' own arms stay in the equation (`V_B·6 m`). Before each rotation's heading, the working draws its **lever arms** (a drawing with `view: arms` and `pivot {node, members}` — the point, and a hinge's part's members, none for the whole structure — in the structure's group; [`render/arms.rs`](../crates/structure/src/render/arms.rs)): the free body of the part balanced about the point (the rest of the structure faint, its loads and reactions left out), the point ringed, and for every force that turns about it its arm as the equation uses it — a vertical force or part by its horizontal distance from the point, in rows below the drawing, a horizontal one by its vertical distance, in columns at its right, an inclined reaction by the perpendicular from the point to its line of action — the shortest nearest the drawing, each with its extension lines and named as the equation names it (`d_(1,A)`, `d_(2,v,A)` in the loads' colour; a reaction's, and an unnamed load's, by its length, `6 m`). A rotation where nothing turns by an arm (only couples) has none. Forces, parts and arms go in the names' order. **Arms are written from what the drawing gives** ([`arms.rs`](../crates/engine/src/cmd/structure/working/arms.rs)): the distances its dimension chains measure (`strix_structure::chains`, whether shown or not) and the loads' lengths, never a centroid's distance the drawing doesn't show — the chain to the load's nearer end plus the part of its length up to the centroid (`(3 m + 0.5 m + (1 m)/2)`: half, a third or two thirds), or the chain to its farther end less the rest when that takes fewer terms (`(6 m − (3 m)/2)`); a load's length as the chains write it (`1.5 m`). A distance no chain measures (along an inclined member) is written by its size. **A hyperstatic structure of degree 1 or 2 finds its redundants by the force method** ([`force.rs`](../crates/engine/src/cmd/structure/working/force.rs)), right after the free body: the redundants are reactions chosen so that, once known, the equilibrium equations give every other one — a roller's or link's reaction first, then a fixed end's couple, then the rest (a propped cantilever releases its roller: a cantilever is left; a continuous beam its middle supports: a simply supported beam) — and their constraints taken away must leave an isostatic **released structure** (`strix_structure::analyse_released`). A line names them (*Hyperstatic structure of degree 1, solved by the force method. Redundant: V_B. …*); then, member by member and stretch by stretch (the stretches of the laws further on: `1a`, `1b`), the released structure's bending moments under the loads (*System 0*, `M_(0,1)(x) = (-180 kN·m) + (60 kN)·x - (5 kN/m)·x^2`) and under each redundant alone equal to 1 (*System 1*, `m_(1,1)(x) = (6 m) - x`, a moment per unit force being a length; `-1 + x/(6 m)` under a unit couple), only where a redundant's law isn't zero; then the congruence — where a constraint was taken away the real structure doesn't move along its redundant — with the displacements of the released structure by the principle of virtual work, `δ_ij = ∫ m_i·m_j dx`, each a sum of calc integrals over the stretches (`δ_11 = ∫_(0 m)^(6 m)▒〖m_(1,1)(x)^2 dx〗 = 72 m³`, `δ_10 = ∫ M_(0,1)(x)·m_(1,1)(x) dx = -1620 kN·m³`), and the equation calc solves with its steps (`0 kN·m^3 = δ_10 + V_B·δ_11 ⇒ … ⇒ V_B = 22.5 kN`); two redundants write both equations (`δ_10 + V_B·δ_11 + V_C·δ_12 = 0`, `δ_20 + V_B·δ_12 + V_C·δ_22 = 0`) and solve them by Cramer's rule (`V_B = (δ_12·δ_20 − δ_10·δ_22)/(δ_11·δ_22 − δ_12^2) =`, with `δ_21 = δ_12`). Without sections `EI` is constant: the coefficients are multiplied by it and the axial strains neglected, as the stiffness solution assumes; a truss integrates its axial forces (`N_(0,1)`, `n_(1,1)`, `EA` constant). Sections given for every member enter as `EI_1 = E·I` (`/EI_1` in the integrals, the coefficients then in `m/kN` and the equation in `m`) and, for a member with an area, `EA_1 = E·A` with its axial forces' term. The other reactions follow by equilibrium from the redundants. Otherwise — three redundants or more, a redundant no reaction gives (a closed frame), sections given for some members only — a reaction no equation isolates, like reactions only found together, is given its value from the stiffness solution, under a line saying so. A note recalls the reactions' signs.
- **Internal forces.** For each member and each stretch between loads: `N_1(x)`, `T_1(x)`, `M_1(x)` (`N_2a`, `N_2b`… when a member has several stretches; the shear's letter is the structure's `titles.v`) as calc functions of `x` from the member's first node, written with the names of the reactions and the loads acting on the part before the section (`T_1(x) = V_A − q_1·x`, `M_1(x) = V_A·x − q_1·x^2/2`, `M_1c(x) = V_A·x − F_1·(x − 2 m) − F_2·(x − 5 m)`, a distributed load the section is past by its resultant, `M_1(x) = V_A·x − M_A`; `crates/engine/src/cmd/structure/working/named.rs`), a reaction that came out negative by its name too, as the stretch's drawing shows it (`H_A = -5 kN`: `N_1a(x) = -H_A`, its value entering when substituted), each checked against the solution's law; a member in a closed ring, a varying load across the section or a law that disagrees is written with its numbers (`(30 kN) − (10 kN/m)·x`). Each stretch opens with its heading (`Stretch 1a: Member 1 (A–B), x from 0 m to 2 m:`) and **a drawing of the stretch** (`view: section`, `cut {member, from, to, tag, action?}`, three quarters of the structure's width, in its group, written again with the working; [`render/section.rs`](../crates/structure/src/render/section.rs)): the stretch marked by a pale band of uniform intensity across both sides of the cut with a tick at each end, the member cut half way along the stretch with a zigzag cut face, the part before the cut (the nodes reached from the member's first node without it, `strix_structure::part_before`) in full — its members, its loads with their names (a distributed load the cut crosses drawn in full up to the cut, unnamed when it varies), its reactions as the laws use them — by name (`H_A`, `V_A`, `M_A`) pointing the way they count positive, one that came out negative too (the reactions' result shows the way it really acts) — and on the cut the internal force(s) the rest puts on it (`N_1a` pulling, `T_1a` turning the part clockwise, `M_1a` stretching the reference fibres, in the panels' colours; all three in a whole working, or the single force `cut.action` asks for in each diagram part of an exercise), its supports faded under their reactions, any loads strictly inside the remainder of the stretch past the cut drawn faded without labels, the rest of the member (and any remaining skeleton) faintly dashed with its far node and support so the whole member is seen without loads past the stretch or reactions past the cut, and the section's distance `x` from the member's first node as a progressive clear of the reactions — a small circle at the origin, a larger arrow at the cut — and below it the progressives of the stretch: the distances from the member's first node to the stretch's ends and to the member's far end (`0`, `2.5 m`, `6 m`), each written after its arrowhead. A member in a closed ring has no part before the cut alone: its stretch is marked on the structure drawn whole, without forces. The laws follow the drawing straight away, with no words: the drawing alone says how they come. They are **boxed**, so they are found at a glance among the values worked out of them: a thin slate border, no shading, set in from the margins, the stretch's laws (all three, or the one an exercise's diagram part asks for) joined in one box and kept together on their page. Then their values at the stretch's ends and at M's extremes under their own heading (`values`: *Values at the ends of the stretch:*, `valuesMax`: *…and at M's extremes:*), one a line, each with its working one step a line under it: the law with `x` substituted, the values substituted, then each term worked out, so a student sees which term weighs what and how they add up (`M_1c(6 m) = V_A·6 m − F_1·(6 m − 1 m) − F_2·sin 60°·(6 m − 4 m)`, `= (27.887 kN)·6 m − …`, `= 167.321 kN·m − 150 kN·m − 17.321 kN·m`, `= 0 kN·m`). The results are rounded to the document's decimals and never in scientific notation (the equations' `count: decimals`): what is left of a balance is 0, not `1.819·10^-14`; a substituted value keeps its digits (`(5·10^-4 kN/mm)`, not `(0.001 kN/mm)`). An exercise draws each stretch in the axial force's, shear's and bending moment's parts with that diagram's internal force alone. A truss gets each member's axial force (`N_3 = (-25 kN)/2`).
- **Pages.** The working is laid out in blocks a page break never splits: a heading (*Working* with the free body, *Loads:*, *Reactions:*, a rotation's `ΣM_A = 0:`, a stretch's `Member 1 (A–B), x from 0 m to 6 m:`) is kept with what follows it, each line of equations with the next line of equations of its block, and a stepped equation's lines together (Word's *keep with next* and *keep lines together*, set on the paragraphs and saved with the document). So when a block doesn't fit at the bottom of a page it starts on the next one, wherever the text above puts it, and the place follows every change; no page break is written. A block taller than a page breaks as usual.
- **Numbers.** Quantities are written exactly when they are simple fractions (`(70 kN)/3`, `(15 m)/8`), so a law evaluated at a stretch's end gives the value itself (`M_2(6 m) = 0 kN·m`), else with eight significant digits; in the document's decimal separator. Lengths in the equations are written as calc writes them in its steps (`6 m`, `(15 m)/8`). Equations start with their zero, `0 kN·m = V_B·6 m − …` (calc solves an equation whose zero has its unit, on either side).
- **Long equations wrap.** A display equation array wider than the line (a calculation's steps one under the other, `{"layout": "lines"}`) wraps row by row at its operators like any long display equation, keeping the rows aligned at `=` as far as the line allows (`crates/layout/src/math/eq_wrap.rs`). Every other equation alone on its line (`T_1a(2.5 m) = … = −5.208 kN`, `M_1c(x) = …`) is in display mode too: wider than the line, it breaks at its operators, preferring a relation, its later lines under the first `=` and spaced 1.5 lines apart (docs/calc.md).
- **It follows the structure.** The working lies between the two ends of a hidden bookmark named after the structure's `group` (`_StrixWorking_structure1_w`; a structure gets a group when its working is first written), and the structure keeps the working's words (`workingLabels`). Every change to the structure or to any drawing of its group — in the Structure Editor, by a `structure.*` command — writes the working again in place, in those words, within the same undo step (`crates/engine/src/cmd/structure/working/live.rs`). While the structure can't be solved (a support removed on the way to another), a line says so (`unsolved`) and the working comes back as soon as it can be solved. Write Working again rewrites it in place rather than adding a second one. What is typed inside the working is lost when it is rewritten; text written after it stays. A change to a calculation parameter the structure uses (`q = 10 kN/m` above it, `q` in a load) rewrites it too, at the next recalculation: the structure keeps a hash of its values as the calculations give them (`workingKey`), and only a change of those values rewrites the working ([`follow_values`](../crates/engine/src/cmd/structure/mode.rs)); a document just opened stays as saved. A working inside a table cell doesn't follow. A labile or invalid structure has no first working (the command says why).

## Exercise

`structure.exercise` (Structures › Structure › **Structure Exercise**, a menu of the templates) writes a new problem exercise ([exercise.md](exercise.md)) after the caret's paragraph, in the interface's language (`questions`: `reactions`, `axial`, `shear`, `zeros`, `maxima`, `moments`, `trussAxial`; `labels`: `title`, `text`, `jump`, `nozero` and the working's labels — `{T}` is the shear's title):

- **Statement:** a line (*Per la struttura in figura, caricata come indicato:*) and under it the structure with its loads, dimensions and angles (`view: structure`).
- **Questions:** a) find the support reactions; b) work out and draw the diagram of N; c) of T; d) find the points where T is zero; e) work out the greatest bending moment at those points (T(x) = 0); f) work out and draw the diagram of M. A truss: a) the reactions; b) the axial force in every member.
- **Solution**, under each repeated question:
  - a) the reactions' working (the loads' names, the equilibrium equations with their steps, the signs' note) and the drawing of the reactions (`view: reactions`);
  - b), c), f) the conventions (under b), then each stretch's law `N_1(x)`, `T_1(x)`, `M_1(x)` with its values at the stretch's ends (a constant N: its law alone; N's stretches end only where an axial load, or a load's axial part, acts: neighbouring stretches with the same law of N are one, `N_1(x)` from 0 m to 6 m under two vertical forces, named afresh over the joined stretch — `N_1a(x) = -H_A` across a crosswise load starting inside it; N's diagram likewise puts its stations and values only where N changes, `MemberResult::breakpoints_of`) and that diagram alone (`view: axial`, `shear`, `moment`);
  - d) for each stretch where T vanishes, its law solved for the place within the stretch: `0 kN = T_1(x_1); 0 m ≤ x_1 ≤ 6 m ⇒ x_1 = 3 m` (the bounds leave out a law of degree two's other root; two places in one stretch are defined, `x_(1,1) = …`, and checked, `T_1(x_(1,1)) = 0 kN`); where T changes sign under a concentrated load, its values on both sides (`T_1a(2 m)`, `T_1b(2 m)`); where it does neither, a line saying so;
  - e) the law of M of each of those stretches and its value there (`M_1(x_1) = 45 kN·m`, `M_1a(2 m)`); with no such point, at the stretch's end where |M| is greatest.

  The equations solving the reactions (and a redundant's value), the places of d) and the moments of e) are marked as their questions' results, so the results summary lists them, each as its name and its value (`d) x_1a = 2.34 m`, `e) M_1a(x_1a) = 23.4 kN·m`), the moment's law and working left to the solution.

The drawings share a `group` (`structure-1`, `structure-2`… one per exercise): a change made to one of them — in the Structure Editor, by a `structure.*` command — is made to the others, each keeping its own view and width. Each question's working lies in its own hidden bookmark (`_StrixWorking_structure1_r`, `_n`, `_t`, `_z`, `_x`, `_m`), and a change made to any drawing writes all of them again, as for [Working](#working) (the results summary follows). Hidden solutions (the student copy) take the reactions and diagrams with them, as any solution.

## Modes

A structure is written in one of two ways: a **technical report** (Structure: the structure with all its diagrams and, when written, its working after it, where the calculations can be written out) or an **exercise on the structure** (Structure Exercise, [above](#exercise)). `structure.mode {mode?: "report"|"exercise", working?, labels?, questions?}` (Structures › To Exercise / To Report; without `mode` it switches to the other) moves the selected structure — or the one of the exercise at the caret — from one to the other, where it was:

- **To an exercise:** its working goes (the exercise writes its own, question by question), then the structure; the exercise takes its place with the same structure (nodes, loads, parameters, diagram settings, the working's words). The calculation parameters it reads (`q = 10 kN/m` above it) become the exercise's data (`q = 10 kN/m`, in full precision), since an exercise's sandbox doesn't see the document's parameters; their definitions stay where they were.
- **To a report:** the exercise goes whole (`exercise.delete`), and the structure takes its place with all its diagrams (`view: all`) and its working (`working: false` leaves it out; a structure that can't be solved there stays without one), in the words the structure kept. Its group stays. The exercise's data (active variant) are written as calculations before it, except those the document already defines there (`kept`: a second `q = …` would read `q`, not define it), which take the document's value.

In either mode the working, when there is one, follows every change to the structure and to the parameters it reads ([Working](#working)). The exercise's title and statement are the defaults (or `labels`): switching back and forth doesn't keep words typed in them.
