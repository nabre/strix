# Plane structures (`strix-structure`)

> **Status:** done — stage 1 (solver, structure object, diagrams, commands), stage 2 (Structures tab, Structure Editor pane with its tables), stage 3 (the written working), stage 4 (dimensions, stations, the full-page editor), the structure exercise and the report/exercise modes · **Last updated:** 2026-11-18 · **Crates:** `crates/structure` (`L1`), `crates/structure/src/working.rs`, `crates/engine/src/cmd/structure.rs` and `cmd/structure/working.rs` (`L4`), `crates/ui-egui/src/structure_pane.rs`, `crates/ui-egui/src/ribbon/structures.rs` (`L6`)

Strix documents hold plane structures — beams, frames and trusses — that are solved where they sit and drawn with their reactions and their N, V (T) and M diagrams one under the other, at the same horizontal scale, as textbooks draw them. Lengths and loads may be calculation parameters (`L`, `q`), so a structure follows the calculations before it and each exercise variant has its own numbers and diagrams. This is a Strix feature beyond Word parity; the model is the one of 2D frame analysis tools such as structural-analyser.com, drawn with the conventions of Italian structural mechanics texts.

## Model (`crates/structure`, L1, depends on `strix-calc` and `strix-plot`)

A `Structure` (JSON, camelCase) has:

- **Units:** `lengthUnit` (`m` default, `cm`, `mm`) and `forceUnit` (`kN` default, `N`, `daN`); moments are force × length, distributed loads force / length. Coordinates are `y` up, angles in degrees counter-clockwise.
- **Nodes** `{id, x, y, hinge?}`: `hinge` makes every member end meeting there free to turn (an internal hinge, Gerber beams).
- **Members** `{id, a, b, hingeA?, hingeB?, e?, i?, area?}`: the local axis `x` goes from `a` to `b`, `y` is turned 90° counter-clockwise; the **reference fibres** are on the `−y` side (below a member drawn left to right, on the right of a column drawn upwards). `hingeA`/`hingeB` release one end. Without `e`, `i`, `area`: `EI = 1` (constant along the structure) and the member does not stretch (`EA = 10⁶·EI/ℓ²`, `ℓ` the longest member), which is what textbook hyperstatic frames assume; isostatic results never depend on stiffness.
- **Supports** `{node, kind, angle?}`: `fixed` (incastro), `pin` (cerniera), `roller` (carrello, a triangle on wheels), `rollerCircle` (the same carrello drawn as a circle under the node on the ground: restrains like a roller), `link` (pendolo: restrains like a roller, drawn as a bar), `slider` (glifo / bipendolo: the translation across its plane and the rotation). `angle` turns the support about its node: at 0 it stands on the ground below the node, at 90 it is on a wall to its right, at −90 on a wall to its left (a cantilever's fixed end).
- **Loads** (`type`):
  - `force {node | member + at, value, angle}` — `angle` −90 (down) by default; `at` from the member's `a` end;
  - `moment {node | member + at, value}` — counter-clockwise positive;
  - `distributed {member, q1, q2?, from?, to?, angle?, axes?, per?}` — linear from `q1` to `q2` (uniform, triangular, trapezoidal) over `[from, to]` (the whole member by default); `axes: "global"` (default; `angle` from the horizontal) or `"local"` (`angle` from the member's axis, −90 towards the reference fibres: wind on a column); `per: "length"` (default, per metre of member) or `"projection"` (per metre of the member's projection across the load: snow on a rafter).
  - **Names** (`load_names`, `crates/structure/src/names.rs`): each load is named by its kind — `F` concentrated forces, `q` distributed loads, `C` couples — and one count runs through all of them (`q_1`, `F_2`, `C_3`…), the loads taken by their place: left to right (a distributed load by its stretch's leftmost end), then top to bottom, then force, distributed load, couple (then as listed); a distributed load that varies names its ends, `q_(1,1)` and `q_(1,2)` (`q₁,₁`, `q₁,₂` on the drawing). A load that does nothing (zero, or nowhere) is skipped. The drawing writes the name before the value (`F₁ = 12 kN`), the working defines the names and its equations use them.
- **Every number is a `Num`**: a JSON number, or an expression of the calculation parameters (`"L/2"`, `"q"`). A parameter with a unit converts to the structure's units (`L = 450 cm` is 4.5 in a metre structure, `q = 12 kN/m` is 12, `30°` is 30 degrees); a plain number counts in the structure's units; `L/2 - 1` mixes a length and a number, so lengths count as plain numbers in the structure's unit there, as coordinates do in a CAD drawing. A failed expression keeps its last value and is reported.
- **Diagrams** `{reactions, n, v, m, values, hatch, signs, dimensions, stations}`: each of `n`, `v`, `m` has `show`, `positive` (`up`: the member's `+y` side; `down`: the reference fibres' side) and `amplitude` (the largest value's height, paper mm, 12 by default). **Italian convention** (default, `structure.set {"convention": "italian"}`): N and V positive up, M positive down, so the moment is drawn on the stretched fibres. `titles {n, v, m}` name the panels (`N`, `T`, `M` by default; `V` for English texts).
- `title`, `width` (paper mm, 160), `answer` (true: reactions and diagrams are the answer, hidden in the student copy).
- `view` (`all` by default, not written; `structure`: the structure with its loads only; `reactions`: the reactions only; `diagrams`: the diagrams only; `axial`, `shear`, `moment`: one diagram only, whatever its flag — a truss's `axial` its axial forces; `equilibrium`: the free body the reactions' working balances, see [Working](#working)) and `group` (a name the drawings of one structure share, `structure-1`…: see [Exercise](#exercise)).

Sign conventions: N positive in tension; V positive when it turns the piece clockwise; M positive when it stretches the reference fibres.

## Analysis

- **Classification** by the rank of the equilibrium matrix (equations: two translations per node and the rotations; unknowns: three internal forces per member — N and the two end moments — and one per constraint): **isostatic**, **hyperstatic of degree h** (unknowns − rank), or **labile** (rank < equations, the number of independent mechanisms). Three hinges in a row, two parallel rollers, a couple on a node where every member is hinged: labile, whatever the count of constraints says.
- **Solution** by the stiffness method: rotations are one per node for the member ends rigidly joined there, one of its own for each hinged end; supports are constraints solved with Lagrange multipliers, which are the reactions. Member loads enter as consistent nodal loads (exact for a clamped beam), and the internal forces along each member follow by equilibrium of the piece from its `a` end, so a stretch's law is exact (polynomial), not sampled from the solver.
- **Truss:** when every member is hinged at both ends and carries no load along it, the structure is a truss: one panel shows each member's axial force (tension positive, coral; compression blue; zero members dashed grey) instead of the N, V, M diagrams.
- **Notable values** (`notables`): at the ends of each stretch (members' ends, loads' positions, with the jumps under concentrated loads), the extremes of M where V is zero, and the zeros of each diagram.

## Drawing

`Structure::render_for(ws, show_answers)` returns a `strix-plot` `PlotRendered`, so the engine draws it, writes its SVG and prints it as it does a plot. Panels, one under the other at one horizontal scale, the ones its `view` shows:

1. the **structure** with its supports (Italian textbook symbols: hatched ground, rollers on wheels, links as bars with hinges, fixed ends hatched), hinges as white circles, node names away from the members, and its **loads** in red: arrows with their names and values (`q₁ = 10 kN/m`, `F₂ = 12 kN`, a varying load's ends `q₃,₁ = 2 kN/m`, `q₃,₂ = 5 kN/m`), curved arrows for couples, distributed loads as rows of arrows proportional to the intensity with their envelope and values;
2. the **reactions** on the structure drawn in grey: components along the axes (along the support's direction for a roller, link or slider), arrows pointing the way the force acts, their magnitudes, curved arrows for moments; a component that came out negative also has, beside its arrow on the side away from the structure, the way it was taken positive (along +x, +y, the roller's direction; a couple counter-clockwise, round the real one) faded and crossed out with a red ✕;
3. **N**, **V (T)** and **M** (or the truss panel), each in its colour (blue, teal, plum), hatched across the axis, with ⊕/⊖ in each area and the notable values written at the curve. Beside each title, the sign convention its values follow on a short piece of member ([`render/conventions.rs`](../crates/structure/src/render/conventions.rs)): N positive pulling (arrows outwards), T positive turning the piece clockwise (up on the left face, down on the right), M positive stretching the reference fibres (dashed below the piece; clockwise on the left face, counter-clockwise on the right).

What the drawing measures (`crates/structure/src/render/annotate.rs`):

- **Dimensions** (`diagrams.dimensions`; Structures › Show › Dimensions; on in a new structure, written out, off when the key is absent, so a structure saved before it keeps its drawing and its size on the page), on the structure's panel: chains of distances in grey below the structure (x) — first with the points where loads act, start and end on level members, when they add any, then between the nodes, then overall when there are more than two stations — and at its right (y, with the loads on plumb members; a grid's cell keeps room for it), texts along their lines. The chains keep clear of everything drawn before them: the first goes 4.5 mm under the lowest support, load, arrow or name (a force pointing up from under the beam, with its name), the chain of heights as far right of the rightmost, and each extension line starts 1 mm under (beside) what is drawn across it, so none crosses a support or a load; loads on an inclined member get a chain parallel to it on its reference fibres' side, distances measured along the member from its first node (as `at`, `from`, `to` are). Each **inclined member's slope** is marked once per value: an arc from a dashed horizontal at its lower end, with the angle. Each **inclined force** (or inclined distributed load) gets the angle it makes with its member: an arc from the member's side nearest the arrow's tail to the arrow, with the angle, in the loads' red; a force along or across its member, or at a node where it runs along or across a member, is evident and gets none.
- **Stations** (`diagrams.stations`; Structures › Show › Stations; on in a new structure, off when absent, as dimensions), on each diagram: a tick on each member's axis where a notable value is inside the member (a load's position, a jump, M's extreme, a zero), a dotted ordinate up to the curve, and the distance from the member's first node (`2.333 m`) written across the axis on the side away from the diagram; one too close to the last on paper keeps only its tick.
- **Values' direction:** a value that belongs to a point (a member's end, a jump, an extreme) is written across the member, standing on the curve there (vertical on a beam); a stretch of constant value keeps one level value at its middle. Rotated texts are `PlotPrim::TextAt` (centred, turned), drawn the same in the document, its SVG and the editor.

A structure that is not solved (labile, invalid) shows the structure and a red line saying why. The student copy (`exercise.show` off, `answer` on) keeps every panel and the structure, with the reactions and diagrams left out, so both copies have the same size.

**Layout.** Each panel is drawn on its own and measured with everything on it — texts (estimated at 0.55 of their height per character), arrows, dimension chains. The scale is the largest that keeps every panel inside `width` (and the structure at most 70 mm tall, 60 in a grid): the structure is drawn, measured and narrowed until nothing sticks out (a reaction's value at the left, the chain of heights at the right), a few rounds at most. The panels are then centred, each column of a grid on its own, and stacked by their real height, 5 mm apart, a diagram's title and sign convention on a row of their own 11 mm tall above it; there are no lines or frames between or round the panels. The student copy is laid out from the teacher's, so both have the same size. Texts keep 1 mm from the line they name; a diagram's values, stations and ⊕/⊖ treat the curves (sampled every 0.8 mm) and each other as obstacles: each tries several places (farther from the curve, a little along the axis either way, a station beside its tick) and takes the first clear one, else the one over the fewest things.

## The Structures tab (Strutture)

Between Math and Teaching (`ribbon::TABS`; a saved ribbon layout gets it after Math). Its groups:

## The Structure Editor pane

A task pane (docked right, 440 pt) for the selected structure, every change made through the `structure.*` commands (so undoable and scriptable):

- **Status:** isostatic, hyperstatic of degree h, labile (with its degrees of freedom), a truss; the expressions that failed and the analysis' warnings.
- **Sheet:** the structure as the document draws it (`Structure::schema_prims`: the same symbols, loads, names, dimensions, slopes and force angles) on a white sheet with a grid; the wheel zooms about the pointer, the middle or right button pans, **Fit** shows it whole. The pointer snaps to the nodes, and to the grid with **Snap** (its step in **Grid**); its coordinates are shown in the corner. **Dimensions** (on by default, the editor's own view: the document draws them as Structures › Show › Dimensions says) shows the chains of dimensions, the inclined members' slopes and the angles inclined forces make with their members, at the same paper size whatever the zoom. Angles are drawn as the dimensions are: a thin grey extension line along the reference (the horizontal, the member), an arc in the dimension lines' colour with an oblique tick at each end, and the angle beside it — on the page too.
- **Full page:** a **double-click** on a structure in the document (or Edit Structure, or the pane's Edit in Full Page) opens the editor **in place of the page**, like the CAD editor: the sheet as large as the window with the typed point below it, the selection's properties and the tables at the right, and the **Structure Editor** ribbon tab brought up (a contextual tab, `structure_pane::EDITOR_TAB`) with the tools: *Selection* (Select, Erase), *Draw* (Node, Member), *Supports* (Fixed, Pin, Roller, Roller (circle), Link, Slider, Internal hinge), *Loads* (Force, Couple, Distributed), *Sheet* (Snap, Grid, Fit, Dimensions), *Teaching* (Write Working) and *Close* (Close Editor) — `ui.structureTool {tool}`, `ui.structureSnap {on?}`, `ui.structureGrid {size}`, `ui.structureFit`, `ui.structureDimensions {on?}`. The pane keeps its own compact toolbar. Close Editor, Esc with nothing selected, or a structure no longer selected (undone, deleted) goes back to the page (which takes the keyboard again) and the tab that was open. The document's panes are hidden meanwhile; one editor takes the page at a time (a CAD drawing does not open over it).
- **Selection** (Select tool): a click picks a node, else a load (its arrow, its couple's circle, its band of arrows), else a member, and shows what the pointer is over; **Shift** or **Ctrl**+click adds or takes out; a drag from an empty place draws a **window**: dragged left to right (blue, solid) it takes the nodes in it, the members with both ends in it and the loads in it; dragged right to left (green, dashed) it takes whatever it touches, members crossing it included (Shift keeps what was selected); a **double-click** selects the whole connected part (its nodes and members); **Ctrl+A** selects every node, member and load. A small tag by the pointer names what it is over with its main figure (`Node A (0; 3 m)`, `Member 1 · 6 m`, `Force · 10 kN · −90°`). **Moving:** dragging a node or a member moves the whole selection (the selected nodes and the ends of the selected members), the node grabbed (a member's end nearest the pointer) landing on the grid when snapping, the Δx, Δy shown in the corner, as one undo step (`structure.set`); a coordinate written as an expression stays one, the move's step kept as one number at its end (`L` → `L + 0.5` → `L + 1`, back to `L`). Dragging a force or couple on a member slides it along the member (its `at`, on the grid; an expression stays one, the same way); a drag that ends where it started changes nothing. The **arrow keys** move the selection by the grid (Shift: ten steps). **Delete** (or Backspace) removes it, nodes with their members, supports and loads, in one step. **Esc** goes one step per press: it cancels the drag or the member being drawn, then goes back to Select, then clears the selection, and only then leaves the full-page editor. The sheet takes these keys (the page and the program's shortcuts do not see them): in the pane once it is clicked, or while the pointer is over it and nothing else has the keyboard; in the full-page editor whenever no box is being typed in, where Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y undo and redo as on the page. Loads are selected by their place in the list, so a change in their number (an undo, a table, a command) takes them out of the selection. Several things selected: the properties say how many, with Remove Selected; one load: its row of the Loads table.
- **Grips** (`structure_pane/grips.rs`): what is selected shows small handles, dragged to edit it graphically in one undo step, the pointer a hand over them. A **node's** square (also at both ends of a selected member; with one node or member selected, since with several a drag moves them all) moves that node alone, onto the grid when snapping (Shift keeps its row or column); dropped on another node (a red ring marks it) it **joins** it: members, support and loads go to that node (which keeps its own support), a member left with no length or doubling another goes. A **point load's** square (a force's point, a couple's centre) carries it onto any node or member (at the point under the pointer, rounded to the grid); a force at a node can be dragged by its arrow the same way. A **force's tail** (round) turns it, by 15° steps when snapping (Shift: freely; without snapping, Shift gives the steps). A **distributed load's** diamonds set where it starts and stops along its member (never shorter than a grid step; at the member's end it is "to the end" again). The corner shows where the grip would land (coordinates, the node or member, the angle, the stretch); expressions stay expressions where the value only moves (`L/2` → `L/2 + 0.5`).
- **Context menu** (a right click, `structure_pane/menu.rs`; a right click on something not selected selects it first; while a member is drawn a right click only ends it; the right button still pans when dragged): on a **node** — Support (the five kinds or None), Internal hinge, **Join the members** (where exactly two members meet: the inverse of Split — the node goes and the two become one, the first along the line keeping its name, section and the other's far-end hinge; the other's loads move onto it, `at` kept as an expression plus the first one's length; a force or couple at the node goes onto the member where the node was; a member running the other way is turned round first, its loads measured from the other end. Only for members on one straight line, with no support or hinge at the node and the same section; otherwise the item is disabled and its tooltip says why), Add a force / couple here, Draw a member from here, Select its loads, Select the connected part, Delete; on a **member** — Add a force / couple here (at the point clicked, on the grid), Add a distributed load, **Split here** / **Split in half** (a new node: the member keeps its name up to it, a new one with the same section goes on, its end hinge moving with the far end; point loads go to the part they are on, `at` kept as an expression less the split; a distributed load across the point becomes two pieces meeting at the value it has there), Hinge at end A / B, Select its ends / loads / the connected part, Delete; on a **force** — Reverse (an expression `F` becomes `-(F)` and back), Direction (Vertical, Horizontal, At 45°, Perpendicular to the member), Delete; on a **couple** — Reverse, Delete; on a **distributed load** — Reverse, Uniform, Triangular, On the whole member, Perpendicular to the member (local axes), Delete; on **several things** — Join the members (two members meeting at a node, as on the node), Select their loads, Deselect, Delete; on the **empty sheet** — Add a node here, Draw a member from here (at the snapped point), Select all, Fit, Snap to the grid, Dimensions. With another tool than Select, Select tool comes first.
- **Tools:** Select (see above), Node, Member (two clicks, then it goes on from its end; Esc or a right click stops; missing nodes are made), the five supports (a click on a node), Internal hinge (a click on a node turns it on or off), Force and Couple (on a node, or on a member at the point clicked, rounded to the grid), Distributed (a uniform load on the member clicked), Erase (a node with what uses it, or a member with its loads). New loads start at 10 in the structure's units.
- **Point:** coordinates typed as in the CAD editor — `x, y`, or `x; y` with decimal commas, or `@dx, dy` from the member's last point or the selected node — place a node (or the member's next point with Member) on Enter.
- **Properties:** in titled sections, label, box and unit lined up. *Node:* name (renamed everywhere), x, y, internal hinge, the members joined there; *Support:* kind and angle; *Loads:* those on the node (a click selects one) and Add Force/Couple there. *Member:* its ends (chosen from the nodes) each with its hinge, its length and slope; *Section:* E, I, A with their units (empty: none); *Loads:* those on the member and Add Force/Couple/Distributed Load on it. *Load:* where it acts, its position, values and direction one per row with their units, then its options. *Several selected:* how many nodes, members and loads, Remove Selected. *Nothing selected:* the structure's title, units of lengths and forces, and how many nodes, members, supports and loads it has.
- **Tables** (closed at first, each with its count): Nodes (x, y, hinge), Members (ends, hinges, E, I, A), Supports (kind, angle), Loads (where, position, values, direction, from the member's axis, per projection), each row editable and removable, with Add buttons. Every number may be a calculation parameter or an expression; a plain number is read with a decimal point or comma.

## Working

`structure.working` (Structures › Teaching › Write Working, or the editor's button) writes after the selected structure its solution as live calculations, in the interface's language (`labels`):

- **Reactions.** One unknown per support component: `H_A`, `V_A` (along x and y), `R_B` along an inclined roller's or link's direction (`H_B` or `V_B` when it is horizontal or vertical), `M_A` for a fixed end or slider. The equations come from equilibrium — rotation about each support (`ΣM_A = 0`), translation along x and y (`ΣF_x = 0`, `ΣF_y = 0`), and the rotation about an internal hinge of the part on one side of it (`ΣM_B = 0 (part about the hinge B, Member 2)`) — and are written in an order where each has one unknown left, so calc solves it and shows its steps one under the other (`{"layout": "lines"}`): `0 kN·m = V_B·6 m − F_1·d_(1,A) ⇒ V_B·6 m = F_1·d_(1,A) ⇒ … ⇒ V_B = 30 kN` (both sides turned so the first unknown's term is positive; an unknown alone with no load is `H_A = 0 kN`). Each reaction solved is a calculation name the later equations use. Under the *Reactions* heading, before the first equation, the working draws the **free body** its equations balance (a drawing of the structure with `view: equilibrium`, in its group, written again with the working; [`render/equilibrium.rs`](../crates/structure/src/render/equilibrium.rs)): the supports faded and their reactions drawn as the unknowns (`H_A`, `V_A`, `V_B`, `R_B`, `M_A`, pointing the way they count positive), each distributed load faded with its resultant at its centroid (`F_1`; a trapezoid's `F_(2,1)` and `F_(2,2)` at a half and two thirds), each askew force faded with its horizontal and vertical parts (`F_(3,h)`, `F_(3,v)`, as long as their share of it), couples by their names, everything named as the equations name it and no values; and the dimensions (whatever Show › Dimensions says) with a station at each resultant, so each moment's arm `d` reads off the drawing. An exercise's reactions part has it too. Loads enter by their names, defined first under *Loads* with their sizes (`F_1 = (12 kN)`, `q_1 = (10 kN/m)`, `q_(2,1) = (2 kN/m)`; the equations' signs give the directions), as resultants. **The quantities are prepared first, in variables of their own**, so each moment equation is written algebraically and stays short: under the loads, each distributed load's resultant `F_n` as its area (`F_1 = q_1·6 m = 60 kN`; a trapezoid as a rectangle and a triangle, `F_(2,1) = q_(2,1)·6 m`, `F_(2,2) = (q_(2,2) − q_(2,1))·(6 m)/2`; a load per projection over the projected length), then each force acting askew — concentrated or a resultant — split into its horizontal and vertical parts (`F_(3,h) = F_3·cos(30°)`, `F_(3,v) = F_3·sin(30°)`; a numeric factor for an angle with no short value), which the translations use too (`ΣF_x`: `H_A − F_(3,h)`). Under each rotation's heading, before its equation, the **arm** of each force about that point, named `d` after the force and the point (`d_(1,A)`, `d_(2,1,A)`, `d_(3,v,A)` for the vertical part's horizontal arm, `d_(3,h,A)` for the horizontal part's vertical one) and worked out at once (`d_(1,B) = 6 m − (3 m)/2 = 4.5 m`), each once (a hinge's part reuses its support's); the equation then reads `0 kN·m = V_B·6 m − F_1·d_(1,A) − F_(3,v)·d_(3,v,A) + C_4`. The reactions' own arms stay in the equation (`V_B·6 m`). Forces, parts and arms go in the names' order. **Arms are written from what the drawing gives** ([`arms.rs`](../crates/engine/src/cmd/structure/working/arms.rs)): the distances its dimension chains measure (`strix_structure::chains`, whether shown or not) and the loads' lengths, never a centroid's distance the drawing doesn't show — the chain to the load's nearer end plus the part of its length up to the centroid (`(3 m + 0.5 m + (1 m)/2)`: half, a third or two thirds), or the chain to its farther end less the rest when that takes fewer terms (`(6 m − (3 m)/2)`); a load's length as the chains write it (`1.5 m`). A distance no chain measures (along an inclined member) is written by its size. A reaction no equation isolates — a hyperstatic structure's redundants, or reactions only found together — is given its value from the stiffness solution, under a line saying so. A note recalls the reactions' signs.
- **Internal forces.** For each member and each stretch between loads: `N_1(x)`, `T_1(x)`, `M_1(x)` (`N_2a`, `N_2b`… when a member has several stretches; the shear's letter is the structure's `titles.v`) as calc functions of `x` from the member's first node, written with the names of the reactions and the loads acting on the part before the section (`T_1(x) = V_A − q_1·x`, `M_1(x) = V_A·x − q_1·x^2/2`, `M_1c(x) = V_A·x − F_1·(x − 2 m) − F_2·(x − 5 m)`, a distributed load the section is past by its resultant, `M_1(x) = V_A·x − M_A`; `crates/engine/src/cmd/structure/working/named.rs`), each checked against the solution's law; a member in a closed ring, a varying load across the section or a law that disagrees is written with its numbers (`(30 kN) − (10 kN/m)·x`). Then their values at the stretch's ends and at M's extremes, one a line (`M_1(3 m) = 45 kN·m`). A truss gets each member's axial force (`N_3 = (-25 kN)/2`).
- **Pages.** The working is laid out in blocks a page break never splits: a heading (*Working*, *Loads:*, *Reactions:* with the free body, a rotation's `ΣM_A = 0:`, a stretch's `Member 1 (A–B), x from 0 m to 6 m:`) is kept with what follows it, each line of equations with the next line of equations of its block, and a stepped equation's lines together (Word's *keep with next* and *keep lines together*, set on the paragraphs and saved with the document). So when a block doesn't fit at the bottom of a page it starts on the next one, wherever the text above puts it, and the place follows every change; no page break is written. A block taller than a page breaks as usual.
- **Numbers.** Quantities are written exactly when they are simple fractions (`(70 kN)/3`, `(15 m)/8`), so a law evaluated at a stretch's end gives the value itself (`M_2(6 m) = 0 kN·m`), else with eight significant digits; in the document's decimal separator. Lengths in the equations are written as calc writes them in its steps (`6 m`, `(15 m)/8`). Equations start with their zero, `0 kN·m = V_B·6 m − …` (calc solves an equation whose zero has its unit, on either side).
- **Long equations wrap.** A display equation array wider than the line (a calculation's steps one under the other, `{"layout": "lines"}`) wraps row by row at its operators like any long display equation, keeping the rows aligned at `=` as far as the line allows (`crates/layout/src/math/eq_wrap.rs`).
- **It follows the structure.** The working lies between the two ends of a hidden bookmark named after the structure's `group` (`_StrixWorking_structure1_w`; a structure gets a group when its working is first written), and the structure keeps the working's words (`workingLabels`). Every change to the structure or to any drawing of its group — in the Structure Editor, by a `structure.*` command — writes the working again in place, in those words, within the same undo step (`crates/engine/src/cmd/structure/working/live.rs`). While the structure can't be solved (a support removed on the way to another), a line says so (`unsolved`) and the working comes back as soon as it can be solved. Write Working again rewrites it in place rather than adding a second one. What is typed inside the working is lost when it is rewritten; text written after it stays. A change to a calculation parameter the structure uses (`q = 10 kN/m` above it, `q` in a load) rewrites it too, at the next recalculation: the structure keeps a hash of its values as the calculations give them (`workingKey`), and only a change of those values rewrites the working ([`follow_values`](../crates/engine/src/cmd/structure/mode.rs)); a document just opened stays as saved. A working inside a table cell doesn't follow. A labile or invalid structure has no first working (the command says why).

## Exercise

`structure.exercise` (Structures › Structure › **Structure Exercise**, a menu of the templates) writes a new problem exercise ([exercise.md](exercise.md)) after the caret's paragraph, in the interface's language (`questions`: `reactions`, `axial`, `shear`, `zeros`, `maxima`, `moments`, `trussAxial`; `labels`: `title`, `text`, `jump`, `nozero` and the working's labels — `{T}` is the shear's title):

- **Statement:** a line (*Per la struttura in figura, caricata come indicato:*) and under it the structure with its loads, dimensions and angles (`view: structure`).
- **Questions:** a) find the support reactions; b) work out and draw the diagram of N; c) of T; d) find the points where T is zero; e) work out the greatest bending moment at those points (T(x) = 0); f) work out and draw the diagram of M. A truss: a) the reactions; b) the axial force in every member.
- **Solution**, under each repeated question:
  - a) the reactions' working (the loads' names, the equilibrium equations with their steps, the signs' note) and the drawing of the reactions (`view: reactions`);
  - b), c), f) the conventions (under b), then each stretch's law `N_1(x)`, `T_1(x)`, `M_1(x)` with its values at the stretch's ends (a constant N: its law alone; N's stretches end only where an axial load, or a load's axial part, acts: neighbouring stretches with the same law of N are one, `N_1(x)` from 0 m to 6 m under two vertical forces) and that diagram alone (`view: axial`, `shear`, `moment`);
  - d) for each stretch where T vanishes, its law solved for the place within the stretch: `0 kN = T_1(x_1); 0 m ≤ x_1 ≤ 6 m ⇒ x_1 = 3 m` (the bounds leave out a law of degree two's other root; two places in one stretch are defined, `x_(1,1) = …`, and checked, `T_1(x_(1,1)) = 0 kN`); where T changes sign under a concentrated load, its values on both sides (`T_1a(2 m)`, `T_1b(2 m)`); where it does neither, a line saying so;
  - e) the law of M of each of those stretches and its value there (`M_1(x_1) = 45 kN·m`, `M_1a(2 m)`); with no such point, at the stretch's end where |M| is greatest.

  The equations solving the reactions (and a redundant's value), the places of d) and the moments of e) are marked as their questions' results, so the results summary lists them.

The drawings share a `group` (`structure-1`, `structure-2`… one per exercise): a change made to one of them — in the Structure Editor, by a `structure.*` command — is made to the others, each keeping its own view and width. Each question's working lies in its own hidden bookmark (`_StrixWorking_structure1_r`, `_n`, `_t`, `_z`, `_x`, `_m`), and a change made to any drawing writes all of them again, as for [Working](#working) (the results summary follows). Hidden solutions (the student copy) take the reactions and diagrams with them, as any solution.

## Modes

A structure is written in one of two ways: a **technical report** (Structure: the structure with all its diagrams and, when written, its working after it, where the calculations can be written out) or an **exercise on the structure** (Structure Exercise, [above](#exercise)). `structure.mode {mode?: "report"|"exercise", working?, labels?, questions?}` (Structures › To Exercise / To Report; without `mode` it switches to the other) moves the selected structure — or the one of the exercise at the caret — from one to the other, where it was:

- **To an exercise:** its working goes (the exercise writes its own, question by question), then the structure; the exercise takes its place with the same structure (nodes, loads, parameters, diagram settings, the working's words). The calculation parameters it reads (`q = 10 kN/m` above it) become the exercise's data (`q = 10 kN/m`, in full precision), since an exercise's sandbox doesn't see the document's parameters; their definitions stay where they were.
- **To a report:** the exercise goes whole (`exercise.delete`), and the structure takes its place with all its diagrams (`view: all`) and its working (`working: false` leaves it out; a structure that can't be solved there stays without one), in the words the structure kept. Its group stays. The exercise's data (active variant) are written as calculations before it, except those the document already defines there (`kept`: a second `q = …` would read `q`, not define it), which take the document's value.

In either mode the working, when there is one, follows every change to the structure and to the parameters it reads ([Working](#working)). The exercise's title and statement are the defaults (or `labels`): switching back and forth doesn't keep words typed in them.
